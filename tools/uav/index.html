Mission solver — start here describe the job; the solver sizes, searches, compares, and reviews
Solve
State the mission and press Solve. The solver sizes a starting airframe from scaling heuristics (payload fraction, wing loading by launch method, power loading), programs the Monte Carlo search below with matching constraints and ranges, runs a wide pass then a refinement pass per candidate configuration, compares configurations when asked, applies the winner to the working design, and writes the full design review. Every choice it makes lands in the visible controls, so you can audit and override any of it.

Sensitivity — what actually moves the design % change in each metric for a +10 % change in one parameter
Each row perturbs one parameter +10 % from its current value (parameters at 0 use +10 % of their range; parameters pinned at their maximum are omitted), holds everything else, and re-runs the model. Bold cells move a metric ≥ 5 % — those are the levers worth arguing about. Values < 0.5 % are muted. This is a local, first-order picture around the current design: move the design, and the sensitivities move too.
Monte Carlo design search uniform sampling / any input or derived value as constraint or objective
1 — parameters to vary (unchecked parameters stay at their current value)
Vary Parameter Current Sample min Sample max
Design parameters default to varied (±30 % around the current value) — uncheck the ones you want to cage. Component-quality and environment inputs (structure/avionics/propulsion mass, efficiencies, Oswald e, pack density, usable fraction, altitude) default off: the optimizer would simply run them to their favorable end, which is optimism, not design insight. Vary them deliberately if that is the question you are asking.
2 — constraints (a sample is feasible when every constraint holds)
Quantity Min allowed Max allowed  
The table starts with the usual suspects: stability and thrust rows carry working bounds; the logistics and mission rows (all-up mass, payload, span, total length, stall speed, endurance) are blank templates — a row with both bounds empty is ignored, so fill in only what the mission actually demands. Handy anchor numbers: 250 g (FAA registration threshold), 25 kg / 55 lb (Part 107 ceiling), the cargo width of your vehicle for span, the workshop door for total length, and a full-flaps jog (≈ 6 m/s) for a hand-catch stall speed.
3 — objectives and run
Objective Direction Weight  
With several objectives, each is min–max normalized over the feasible samples (flipped for minimize), then combined as a weighted average into a 0–1 score; the best design maximizes that score. Weights set where on the trade curve you land — e.g. endurance and payload both maximized at equal weight picks the knee, weight one of them heavier to slide along the curve. Knee-curve plots for every objective pair are generated automatically below.

After a run, plots generate automatically: with several objectives, a knee-curve scatter for each objective pair comes first (raw units — the feasible cloud is the attainable trade), then every varied parameter gets its own scatter against the objective (single) or the weighted score (multiple). Accent dots satisfy all constraints, gray dots violate at least one, the ringed dot is the chosen best.

Model notes — assumptions and formulas
Scope. Conceptual-design sizing for conventional-layout electric fixed-wing aircraft, from 300 g park flyers to the ≈100 kg class. Estimates are for comparing configurations, not certifying one — expect ±15–25 % against a built airframe. Note for heavy designs: the airfoil polar summaries are low-Reynolds measurements, so at the multi-million Reynolds numbers of large airframes the drag figures run conservative (real sections do somewhat better), and regulatory/structural questions this tool does not touch start to dominate the design. Planform. Trapezoidal wing: S = b·cₙ(1+λ)/2, AR = b²/S, MAC = ⅔·cₙ·(1+λ+λ²)/(1+λ). Tail surfaces are sized from the chosen volume coefficients: Sₚ = Vₚ·S·MAC/lₜ, Sₛ = Vₛ·S·b/lₜ. Lift. 3-D lift-curve slope a = a₀/(1 + a₀/(πeAR)); CL max is the 2-D section value knocked down 10 % for the finite wing. Stall speed from Vₛ = √(2W/(ρSCL max)). Drag. Parasite buildup referenced to wing area: section cd₀ + fuselage (0.006·Sₜₒₜ/S with Sₜₒₜ ≈ 0.8·πDL) + tails (0.008·(Sₚ+Sₛ)/S), all ×1.15 for interference; induced drag CL²/(πeAR). L/D max = 1/(2√(kCD₀)). Propulsion. Electrical power to thrust power through motor+ESC efficiency and a cruise prop efficiency you set. Static thrust from actuator-disk theory with a 0.7 static figure of merit: T = 0.7·(2ρA)^⅓·Pₛₚₐₖₜ^⅔. Climb rate is excess power over weight at the minimum-power speed. Battery. Pack mass from capacity × cells × 3.7 V ÷ energy density. Endurance uses the usable-capacity fraction at the cruise current; range is still-air. Stability. Neutral point from tail-volume method: xₙₒ/MAC = 0.25 + 0.9·Vₚ·(aₜ/a₝)·(1−dε/dα) − 0.04 (fuselage correction), with dε/dα = 2a₝/(πAR). The tail lift-curve slope aₜ comes from the configured tail aspect ratio; a T-tail sits above the wake and is modeled with a 30 % lower downwash gradient. Static margin is NP minus CG in % MAC. Tail configurations. Tail surfaces are sized from the volume coefficients, then shaped by the tail aspect ratios and taper. A V-tail uses the classic conversion: total panel area = Sₚ + Sₛ, panel dihedral Γ = atan √(Sₛ/Sₚ) — drag and stability use the projected areas, so a V-tail here is aerodynamically equivalent to the conventional tail it replaces (the real-world differences: fewer junctions, ruddervator mixing, pitch/yaw coupling, are not modeled). Flying wing / BWB. No tail surfaces: the neutral point is the wing aerodynamic center (25 % MAC, less a 2 % blended-body correction), so the CG must sit ahead of ≈23 % MAC and static margins run 3–8 %. Trim comes from reflex and washout, which costs ≈15 % of CL max (applied) and demands a near-zero section pitching moment — the checks flag cambered sections. Winglets are carried at a fixed 8 % of wing area for drag and drawing; yaw stiffness from sweep + winglets is not modeled quantitatively. Elevon sizing, washout distribution, and the deep BWB center-body lift carryover are beyond this fidelity. Mission solver. Initial sizing uses scaling heuristics — AUW from a 28 % payload fraction, wing loading by launch method (35/55/85 g/dm² hand/catapult/runway), power from an L/D≈9–10 cruise estimate and a thrust-to-weight floor, motor and cells snapped to the nearest COTS preset class, Li-ion chemistry above 90 min targets. The heuristic only centers the search; the Monte Carlo stages (wide, then re-centered ±18 % of the range around the stage-1 best) do the actual optimization against the mission constraints. Structure mass is held at a fixed fraction of the initial AUW estimate — refine it from build data, it is the weakest number in the loop. COTS presets. The airframe/motor/battery tables carry approximate class-typical specs, labeled by size class rather than brand. They are starting points for realistic combinations, not vendor data — check the actual datasheet before you buy or fly. Not modeled. Compressibility, wind, control-surface sizing, structural sizing/flutter, prop maps vs advance ratio, trim drag, battery voltage sag. Airfoil polar values are single-point summaries of low-Reynolds published data; outside Re ≈ 60k–500k they drift. Roadmap for this lab. Constraint-driven inverse solving, prop/motor database with advance-ratio maps, structural weight estimation from geometry, multi-point missions (climb + cruise + loiter), and design-file schema v2 with named mission segments.
