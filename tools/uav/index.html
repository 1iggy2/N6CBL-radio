Model notes — assumptions and formulas
Scope. Conceptual-design sizing for conventional-layout electric fixed-wing aircraft in the 0.3–20 kg class. Estimates are for comparing configurations, not certifying one — expect ±15–25 % against a built airframe. Planform. Trapezoidal wing: S = b·cₙ(1+λ)/2, AR = b²/S, MAC = ⅔·cₙ·(1+λ+λ²)/(1+λ). Tail surfaces are sized from the chosen volume coefficients: Sₚ = Vₚ·S·MAC/lₜ, Sₛ = Vₛ·S·b/lₜ. Lift. 3-D lift-curve slope a = a₀/(1 + a₀/(πeAR)); CL max is the 2-D section value knocked down 10 % for the finite wing. Stall speed from Vₛ = √(2W/(ρSCL max)). Drag. Parasite buildup referenced to wing area: section cd₀ + fuselage (0.006·Sₜₒₜ/S with Sₜₒₜ ≈ 0.8·πDL) + tails (0.008·(Sₚ+Sₛ)/S), all ×1.15 for interference; induced drag CL²/(πeAR). L/D max = 1/(2√(kCD₀)). Propulsion. Electrical power to thrust power through motor+ESC efficiency and a cruise prop efficiency you set. Static thrust from actuator-disk theory with a 0.7 static figure of merit: T = 0.7·(2ρA)^⅓·Pₛₚₐₖₜ^⅔. Climb rate is excess power over weight at the minimum-power speed. Battery. Pack mass from capacity × cells × 3.7 V ÷ energy density. Endurance uses the usable-capacity fraction at the cruise current; range is still-air. Stability. Neutral point from tail-volume method: xₙₒ/MAC = 0.25 + 0.9·Vₚ·(aₜ/a₝)·(1−dε/dα) − 0.04 (fuselage correction), with dε/dα = 2a₝/(πAR). The tail lift-curve slope aₜ comes from the configured tail aspect ratio; a T-tail sits above the wake and is modeled with a 30 % lower downwash gradient. Static margin is NP minus CG in % MAC. Tail configurations. Tail surfaces are sized from the volume coefficients, then shaped by the tail aspect ratios and taper. A V-tail uses the classic conversion: total panel area = Sₚ + Sₛ, panel dihedral Γ = atan √(Sₛ/Sₚ) — drag and stability use the projected areas, so a V-tail here is aerodynamically equivalent to the conventional tail it replaces (the real-world differences: fewer junctions, ruddervator mixing, pitch/yaw coupling, are not modeled). COTS presets. The airframe/motor/battery tables carry approximate class-typical specs, labeled by size class rather than brand. They are starting points for realistic combinations, not vendor data — check the actual datasheet before you buy or fly. Not modeled. Compressibility, wind, control-surface sizing, structural sizing/flutter, prop maps vs advance ratio, trim drag, battery voltage sag. Airfoil polar values are single-point summaries of low-Reynolds published data; outside Re ≈ 60k–500k they drift. Roadmap for this lab. Constraint-driven inverse solving, prop/motor database with advance-ratio maps, structural weight estimation from geometry, multi-point missions (climb + cruise + loiter), and design-file schema v2 with named mission segments.
